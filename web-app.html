GlycoTrace, straight from your browser

Log your glucose, food, enzymes, insulin and exercise on any device with a browser. That includes iPhone and iPad, where there is no App Store version yet. Same account, same data, nothing to download.

If you are on an iPhone, you do not have to wait

The native iOS app is still in development. That is the honest position, and for a long time it meant anyone on an iPhone simply could not use GlycoTrace. The web app closes that gap. It opens in Safari, Chrome or any modern browser, it handles full logging rather than a cut-down view, and you can create an account and start today.

On an iPhone, open the web app in Safari, tap the share icon, then choose Add to Home Screen. It gets its own icon and opens without the browser chrome, so day to day it behaves much like an installed app.

What you can log in the browser

Everything the daily log needs, not a read-only summary.

Glucose

Readings in mmol/L, plotted across 7, 14, 30 or 90 days with time in range and your average.

Food and carbs

Search your recent foods, common staples or the Open Food Facts database, then log carbohydrates, protein, fat and calories.

PERT enzymes

Record Creon and other enzyme doses against the meal they belong to, so the two are never separated.

Insulin

Rapid-acting and long-acting logged separately, with the time you actually took the dose.

Exercise and weight

Activity type and duration, plus weight over time, both sitting alongside your glucose rather than in another app.

Medication and appointments

Keep doses and upcoming clinic dates in the same record as the data you will be discussing.

CGM CSV import

Upload a glucose export from LibreView or a similar service. The imported readings sync to your phone too.

History and goals

Review past entries, correct anything you got wrong, and set weekly targets for calories, carbs, protein and fat.

One account, both places

The web app and the Android app are two front doors to the same record. They share one login and one database, so a meal logged on your laptop at work is on your phone before you get home, and a reading taken on your phone is there when you sit down to review the week on a bigger screen.

Reviewing a month of data is genuinely easier on a laptop than on a handset, which is worth knowing before your next clinic appointment.

What the Android app still does better

It would be misleading to claim the browser version matches the phone app feature for feature. It does not, and some things genuinely need a phone. The Android app adds:

  • Barcode scanning for packaged food, which needs a camera
  • AI food estimation and voice entry for logging a meal without typing it out
  • Automatic LibreLink sync, rather than importing a CSV yourself
  • Insights and pattern analysis across your logged history
  • Clinical PDF reports to take to appointments
  • Reminders and a home-screen widget

If you have an Android phone, use that as your main log and treat the browser as the comfortable place to review. If you are on iPhone, the web app is the whole product for now, and your data will be waiting when the iOS version lands.

Web app: common questions

Is there a GlycoTrace app for iPhone?

Not on the App Store yet, but the web app runs on iPhone and iPad today and supports full logging. Add it to your home screen and it opens like an app.

Do I need the Android app first?

No. You can create a new account directly in the browser with an email address, or sign in with Google. The Android app is optional.

Is it the same data as my phone?

Yes. One account, one database. Sign in with the same details and everything is there in both places.

Can I import my FreeStyle Libre data?

Yes. Upload a glucose CSV export, for example from LibreView. It reads the glucose and timestamp columns and ignores everything else.

Does it cost anything?

No. Creating an account and logging your data in the browser is free.
