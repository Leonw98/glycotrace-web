## Strategy decision (2026-06-15): "mini expand, not compete"
The GlycoTrace app already serves weight/fitness alongside multiple diabetes
types, so this content is legitimate for the product. But we do NOT compete on
generic head terms ("diabetic fat loss", "calorie deficit") owned by giants
(Diabetes UK, MyFitnessPal, big fitness sites). We win where fitness meets our
owned niche and Leon's lived experience, on long-tail, specific intent:
- Anchor to the **Type 3c / EPI crossover** wherever possible (weight and muscle
  with malabsorption is genuinely under-served white space, and on-niche).
- Lead with **CGM data** and **real-world glucose patterns** (the app's job).
- Frame as **lived experience + sourced evidence**, which is strong E-E-A-T and
  is the one thing the giants cannot copy.

## Safety rails (YMYL, blocking, non-negotiable)
- **No prescriptive insulin advice, ever.** Basal reduction as a fat-loss
  "master key", night-basal timing, the "next-day vacuum" all come out as
  how-to. Insulin may only appear as informational context with an explicit
  "discuss any dose change with your diabetes team". HARD STOP otherwise.
- Every biological claim ("liver dump", "CNS 72-hour reset", "anabolic window",
  protein ratios) needs a citation, or is clearly labelled "in my experience".
- Include the standard safety note: do not exercise with high blood glucose or
  ketones; this is education, not medical advice.

## Original outline (verbatim, from Gemini)

### Content Pillar 1: Diabetic Fat Loss & The Calorie Deficit
Target Keyword/Concept: Diabetic fat loss, calorie deficit with insulin, lowering basal insulin.
- The "Feeding the Insulin" Trap: Why cutting calories often leads to hypos, and why adjusting background (basal) insulin is the biological master key to unlocking fat cells without eating rescue carbs.
- The Protein Myth for Diabetic Athletes: Why aiming for bodybuilder protein targets (like 160g+) is unnecessary, and how the 1.6g/kg ratio frees up calories to protect the deficit.
- The "Rice Cake Rollercoaster": The danger of grazing on fast-acting carbs throughout the day, and how a slow drip of glucose locks the fat-burning doors shut.

### Content Pillar 2: Real-World Activity & Blood Sugar Tracking
Target Keyword/Concept: NEAT calorie burn, liver dumps, CGM data interpretation, insulin timing.
- The "Liver Dump" Phenomenon: Why fasted manual labour (heavy DIY) causes blood sugar to drift into 13.0+ mmol/L due to stress hormones, not food.
- Why Your Fitness Tracker is Blind to Manual Labour: The flaw in relying on heart-rate monitors (Garmin) to track the calorie burn of slow isometric tension (NEAT).
- The "Next-Day Vacuum": How injecting long-acting basal at night can create an active insulin peak the following day, dragging blood sugar low during physical work.
- The "Big Meal Rebound": Why one massive meal after a long fast causes slow digestion and quiet overnight spikes.

### Content Pillar 3: Athletic Recovery & Programming (Over 40)
Target Keyword/Concept: Diabetic muscle building over 40, CNS fatigue, hybrid lifting, sports recovery.
- CNS Fatigue vs Muscle Fatigue: Why the CNS needs a 72-hour reset after back-to-back matches, and why heavy 5x5 on a drained battery spikes cortisol.
- Structuring a Hybrid Lifting Week: balancing heavy mechanical tension (low-rep strength) with metabolic stress (supersets) to build muscle without destroying joints.
- The Hidden Joint Tax of Court Sports: the difference between the cardiovascular drain of football and the plyometric, tendon-heavy impact of badminton.
- The Midnight "Drip-Feed" Protocol: debunking the immediate anabolic window and using a zero-carb, high-protein snack before bed to fuel overnight repair while keeping blood sugar flat.

### Content Pillar 4: Mindset & System Management
Target Keyword/Concept: Overtraining, diet fatigue, CGM Time-in-Range (TIR).
- The Tactical Retreat: why dropping exercise volume to focus on dietary prep and insulin timing is the fastest way to break a fat-loss plateau.
- Reading Your CGM Data Without Panicking: the difference between short-term performance friction (stalled fat loss) and actual long-term biological damage when reviewing a messy Time-in-Range graph.
